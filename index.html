01 Project overview

Healthcare is more than a doctor's appointment.

A treatment journey can include onboarding, medication, follow-ups, progress tracking, questions, and decisions. This concept explores how those moments might feel more connected through one patient-centered experience.

03 Synthesis

From product research to design opportunities.

Across the three products, I identified recurring themes that could guide the design of a clearer treatment journey.

Translate complexity

Healthcare information and workflows need to become understandable actions, not more information for patients to interpret alone.

Care continues over time

Patients may need support between consultations: reminders, check-ins, progress context, and clarity about what happens next.

AI needs human context

AI-supported guidance should communicate its limits and help people reach qualified human support when needed.

Personalize carefully

Relevant health context can make guidance more useful, while privacy, consent, and clear explanations remain essential.

Make progress visible

Milestones, completed actions, and upcoming steps can help patients understand where they are in a longer journey.

@

Different needs, one system

A product needs consistent navigation and patterns while allowing care journeys to differ by condition and treatment plan.

04 Design direction

Make the next step feel clear.

The concept focuses on the patient-facing treatment journey rather than attempting to design an entire healthcare platform.

Problem statement

Patients using digital healthcare services can find it difficult to understand their overall treatment journey, know what they need to do next, and navigate between AI guidance and human support.

How might we?

How might we make an ongoing healthcare journey clearer and easier to navigate, while helping patients understand their next steps and access the right support at the right time?

Product scope

What this concept will focus on

Helping patients understand their current treatment stage, identify their next steps, receive understandable guidance, track progress, and access appropriate support.

06 Feature exploration

Additional ways to make care feel connected.

Feature 01. Records & medication

Prescription Upload

Let patients upload a prescription and keep it connected to their treatment information, reducing the need to search across chats, folders, and paper documents.

  • Upload or photograph a prescription.
  • View the document and its upload date.
  • Keep prescription details available alongside medication information.
Design considerations: make document capture simple, show upload status, protect sensitive information, and avoid interpreting prescriptions without a qualified review. Any extracted details should be clearly marked for confirmation.

Feature 02. Care & safety

Wound & Skin Concern Upload

Allow patients to capture and upload photos of wound healing or skin conditions to track changes over time and share visual updates securely with their care team.

  • High-resolution image capture with guided framing.
  • Log visual updates to a chronological progress timeline.
  • Share visual data securely with clinicians for remote review.
Design considerations: provide clear lighting and positioning guidelines for consistent photo capture, ensure HIPAA/GDPR-compliant encrypted media handling, and avoid providing automated diagnostic promises without clinical oversight.

Feature 03. Guidance & family

Family Emergency Contacts

Users can add trusted family members or emergency contacts so they can quickly reach them during an emergency.

  • Add and update trusted contacts.
  • Choose contact relationships and preferred phone numbers.
  • Understand when and how a contact could be notified.
Design considerations: verify contact details, explain notification behavior before activation, let users remove contacts, and do not share health information with a contact without appropriate permission.

Feature 04. Care & safety

Emergency Alert

A dedicated emergency option that allows users to quickly contact ambulance or emergency services when urgent assistance is required.

  • Make the alert action easy to understand.
  • Confirm which contacts and details are included.
  • Show a clear status after the alert is triggered.
Design considerations: show the exact action before it happens, provide feedback if delivery fails, and never suggest an alert was sent when it was not. Location sharing requires permission and clear disclosure.

Feature 05. Records & medication

Pharmacy & Medication

Users can purchase their prescribed or eligible medications directly through the app. The pharmacy feature can allow users to search for medicines, view medication details, upload or connect prescriptions, add medicines to their cart, and place orders for delivery.

  • Search for medicines and review verified details.
  • Connect an eligible prescription when required.
  • Review a cart, delivery details, and order status.
Design considerations: use verified medicine information, communicate availability and delivery terms clearly, protect prescription data, and make pharmacist or clinician support accessible when questions require professional advice.

Feature 06. Guidance & family

AI Health Guidance

An AI-assisted guidance feature where users can ask about common health concerns and receive understandable information and possible next steps. Examples could include menstrual cramps, knee or joint pain, and nutrition-related questions.

  • Ask about common concerns such as cramps or joint pain.
  • Explain health information in plain language.
  • Identify when professional or urgent care may be needed.
Design considerations: communicate uncertainty, avoid presenting the AI as a diagnosis, protect sensitive conversation data, and use clear escalation guidance. High-risk symptoms should not be handled as routine self-care advice.

Feature 07. Guidance & family

Food & Nutrition Suggestions

The experience can provide general food and nutrition suggestions based on the user's health-related questions and goals, while clearly distinguishing general guidance from personalized medical advice.

  • Browse practical meal and food ideas.
  • Explore suggestions around stated goals or preferences.
  • Explain the limits of general nutrition guidance.
Design considerations: account for allergies, cultural preferences, accessibility, and relevant medical constraints. Avoid unsupported claims, extreme recommendations, or treating general information as a personalized clinical plan.

Feature 08. Guidance & family

Family & Kids Health Space

Help people keep family health information organized through separate profiles, with carefully managed access for trusted collaborators.

  • Add health profiles for partner, children, and parents.
  • Store basic medical information, medications, allergies, prescriptions, and health records.
  • Add a collaborator via email and set permissions for what information they can access.
Design considerations: separate each person's records, establish age-appropriate consent and guardian permissions, provide clear access controls, and make it easy to review or revoke collaborator access.

07 Design principles

Trust is part of the interface.

Healthcare design needs to account for moments when people may feel worried, rushed, or unfamiliar with medical language. The interface should make information and actions easier to understand, while respecting the limits of digital guidance.

Clarity

Make the current status and next step easy to understand.

Trust & transparency

Explain what AI can and cannot do, and make uncertainty visible.

Patient control

Help people understand their choices and manage their information.

Safety

Make appropriate clinical or emergency support easy to find.

Accessibility

Use plain language, readable type, and accessible interactions.

Continuity

Connect actions, progress, records, and support over time.

Reflection & next steps

The next step is to validate these assumptions with patients and healthcare professionals, prioritize the most valuable feature, and test early prototypes for clarity, accessibility, and trust.

This project represents my current understanding and exploration of the problem space. I am open to feedback, new evidence, and adapting the research or design direction to real product needs.
